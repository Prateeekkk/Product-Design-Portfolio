About

Designer who works close
to the product.

Prateek · Bengaluru 4 yrs · Product Design

The best UI is the one no one notices. I keep failing at this in interesting ways.

I started in design making landing pages for friends and slowly realising the landing page wasn't the problem. Four years in, I'm still figuring out the same thing — just at higher resolution, and on bigger surfaces.

I work best on messy, end-to-end product surfaces where the question isn't "what should this button look like" but "should this button exist at all." Fintech, internal tools, anything with a workflow that someone has been quietly tolerating for too long.

AI is part of the loop, not a label. I prototype closer to the real product instead of static screens — so flows get tested earlier and edge cases stop being surprises in QA.

Things I believe, mostly.

  1. If the copy is wrong, the design is wrong. The button doesn't fix it.
  2. "Make it pop" is feedback the same way "I'm fine" is.
  3. Most products don't need more features. They need fewer, better ones.
  4. Engineers are not a downstream. They are the design team.
  5. If you can't explain the flow on a napkin, the user can't follow it on a screen.

AI in my workflow

Figma + AI, in the same loop.

AI doesn't replace the design work — it tightens the loop around it. Four stages where it actually changes how I ship.

  1. 01 Frame

    Turn rambles into a brief.

    Drop a Slack thread, a Loom transcript, or a PM voice note into Claude with a structured prompt. Out comes a one-pager: problem, users, constraints, success signal. I edit, push back, and kill the wrong assumptions before opening Figma.

    Claude Notion Saves a half-day of "what are we even building"
  2. 02 Prototype

    Prototype closer to the real thing.

    For a flow I'd usually static-mock in Figma, I scaffold a working HTML prototype with Cursor — real states, real validation, real data shape. Reviewers click instead of imagining. Engineers see interactions, not interpretations.

    Cursor Claude Figma Working clickable, not just hi-fi
  3. 03 Stress-test

    Find edge cases before QA does.

    I ask the model to play hostile user — empty states, partial data, network drops, currency mismatches, role permissions. The answers aren't gospel; they're a checklist. Most of the bugs I used to find in QA, I now design around two days earlier.

    Claude Perplexity Edge cases shift left, into design
  4. 04 Hand off

    Specs that engineers don't have to translate.

    Design tokens, component states, and copy variants exported as a structured spec — not a screenshot dump. Engineers get values they can paste, not pixels they have to measure. Handoff arguments became handoff comments.

    Figma Cursor Notion Cleaner handoff, fewer round-trips
